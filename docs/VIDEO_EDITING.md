# Free Video Editing Software for Studio OS

Studio OS generates short AI clips. These free tools are recommended for assembling, finishing, and polishing them into final videos.

All options below are free. Most are fully open-source. They fit the project's goal of virtually zero ongoing cost.

---

## Top Recommendations

### 1. DaVinci Resolve (Free Version) — Best Overall
- **License**: Free (proprietary but fully usable free edition)
- **Platforms**: Windows, macOS, Linux
- **Best for**: Color grading, audio mixing, professional finishing, YouTube/series work
- **Why**: The free version has almost no limitations for most creators. Excellent color tools, Fairlight audio, and a powerful timeline. No watermark.
- **Download**: blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve

### 2. Shotcut — Best Lightweight Open Source
- **License**: GPL (fully open source)
- **Platforms**: Windows, macOS, Linux
- **Best for**: Older or weaker computers, simple-to-medium projects, cross-platform consistency
- **Why**: Actively maintained, no watermark, good format support, proxy editing.
- **Download**: shotcut.org

### 3. Kdenlive — Best Full Open-Source Timeline Editor
- **License**: GPL (fully open source)
- **Platforms**: Windows, macOS, Linux, BSD
- **Best for**: Multi-track editing, proxy workflows, users who want a traditional NLE experience
- **Why**: Mature, customizable, active KDE development, solid effects and titles.
- **Download**: kdenlive.org

### 4. OpenShot — Best for Beginners
- **License**: GPL (fully open source)
- **Platforms**: Windows, macOS, Linux
- **Best for**: First-time editors, quick simple edits, 3D titles
- **Why**: Drag-and-drop interface, easy to learn, plenty of transitions.
- **Download**: openshot.org

### 5. LosslessCut — Best for Quick Cuts
- **License**: GPL (open source)
- **Platforms**: Windows, macOS, Linux
- **Best for**: Trimming, splitting, or joining clips without quality loss
- **Why**: Extremely fast because it does not re-encode. Perfect for cleaning up generated clips before full editing.
- **Download**: github.com/mifi/lossless-cut

### 6. Blender — Best for VFX and Motion Graphics
- **License**: GPL (fully open source)
- **Platforms**: Windows, macOS, Linux
- **Best for**: Advanced compositing, 3D elements, motion graphics, tracking
- **Why**: Extremely powerful if you need more than basic editing.
- **Download**: blender.org

### 7. FFmpeg — Best for Command-Line / Batch Work
- **License**: LGPL/GPL (open source)
- **Platforms**: Everything
- **Best for**: Concatenating clips, converting formats, basic automated edits, scripting
- **Why**: The underlying engine most editors use. Excellent for repeatable pipelines.
- **Download**: ffmpeg.org

## Quick Start Suggestions

| Your situation | Start with |
|----------------|------------|
| Just want to join a few clips and add music | OpenShot or Shotcut |
| Serious YouTube / series finishing | DaVinci Resolve (free) |
| Prefer pure open-source only | Kdenlive or Shotcut |
| Need to trim many short clips fast | LosslessCut then Resolve |
| Advanced effects or 3D | Blender + Kdenlive |

---

## Workflow with Studio OS

1. Generate short clips in Studio OS (with character lock).
2. Export / download the clips.
3. Import into your chosen editor.
4. Arrange, add audio, titles, color grade.
5. Export final video (H.264 or H.265 recommended for YouTube).
6. (Optional) Archive both the raw Studio OS clips and the finished edit.

---

## Notes

- All listed tools are free to use commercially.
- Prefer local editing to stay at zero cost and keep files private.
- DaVinci Resolve free is the strongest all-in-one option for most creators even though it is not fully open-source.
- For pure open-source purity: Kdenlive or Shotcut.
